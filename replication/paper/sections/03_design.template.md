## Data Collection

**Models.** We study three open instruction-tuned models of similar size from different developers: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen Team, 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Llama Team, 2024), and OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct (OLMo Team, 2025). All three run locally in 8-bit floating point (FP8). Every quantity we analyze is read from the models' next-token probabilities or hidden activations.

**Economic and social directions.** To place legislators in a two-dimensional space of the model's own making, we extract an economic and a social direction from each model by adapting the persona-vector procedure of Chen et al. (2025, pp. 3–4). The model answers the same policy questions under opposing system instructions, economically left against economically right and socially progressive against socially traditional, and each direction is the difference between the mean activations of the two sets of answers. Activations are the internal numerical values that a model computes at each of its successive processing stages, called layers. The two directions are then made perpendicular by a symmetric orthogonalization, so that neither axis takes priority. As in Chen et al. (2025, p. 4), the layer is the one at which the directions steer the model's answers most effectively (adding a direction to the activations moves the answers furthest toward the intended side), as rated by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for all three models (the judge ranks layers only and scores none of the reported outcomes): layer {{layer_qwen}} in Qwen, {{layer_llama}} in Llama, and {{layer_olmo}} in OLMo (see Appendix A for the questions, the instructions, and the selection rule). Steering is used only to select the layer. None of the analyses below adds anything to the models' activations.

**Legislator map.** We prompt each model to answer as every member of the House and Senate from the 80th to the 118th Congress, {{n_map_rows}} member-Congress rows in all. The prompt gives the member's name, chamber, state, and year, but not the member's party. For a set of held-out economic and social questions, we project the model's activations onto the two directions and subtract the projection obtained without the persona, so that the origin of the map is the model's default position. The result is an economic and a social coordinate for every member in every Congress (see Appendix A for the prompt). To know which members the model recognizes, we also ask it for the member's party; it answers correctly for {{recall_range}} of rows.

**Roll-call benchmark.** The benchmark is each member's own voting record, not an aggregate score. We build an economic and an abortion scale for every Congress and chamber from Voteview roll calls (Lewis et al., 2026). Economic roll calls are those that the Policy Agendas Project codes under macroeconomics, labor, education, environment, or social welfare (Jones et al., 2025), and abortion roll calls are identified from Voteview's vote descriptions. Abortion serves as the social benchmark because its roll calls span the whole period and party positions on it changed during that period (Adams, 1997). Each scale is the first principal component of the members' votes on these roll calls, oriented by its correlation with the first DW-NOMINATE dimension or, where that correlation is weak, by the position of Southern Democrats (see Appendix A). Splitting both scales at their medians within each Congress and chamber assigns every member-Congress row to a voting quadrant. Members who are economically left and socially progressive (EL-SP) or economically right and socially traditional (ER-ST) are aligned with the usual party combinations. Members who are economically left and socially traditional (EL-ST) or economically right and socially progressive (ER-SP) are cross-pressured. Abortion scales exist for 20 Congresses in each chamber between the 93rd (1973) and the 117th (2021). In those Congresses, {{n_rows}} Democratic and Republican rows have both scales, and {{cross_share}} of them are cross-pressured.

## Analysis

We run three analyses, each aimed at one part of the question set out in Section 2.

**Map and votes.** To test whether the map recovers cross-pressured members, we split the model's two coordinates at their medians in the same way and compare each row's map quadrant with its voting quadrant. The main quantity is recall by voting quadrant: the share of rows in a quadrant that the map places in that same quadrant. Row-level recall counts a member once for every Congress served and includes many members close to the medians. We therefore also select {{a12_n}} members per voting quadrant by their votes, among members whose party Qwen and Llama recognize, most of them far from both medians, and count how many the map places in their own quadrant (see Appendix A for the selection).

**Stated profiles.** To test the missing-information explanation, we state a profile in the prompt instead of leaving the model to infer it. The four profiles correspond to the four quadrants, and each is given in five ways, from a short description of an anonymous House member's positions to the name of a real member whose votes place them in that quadrant ({{s5_n}} conditions per model). We compare cross-pressured with aligned profiles on four outcomes: how consistently the model answers single-issue questions in line with the profile, whether the profile's projection reaches its quadrant, and the uncertainty of the model's choices and of its generated text. The tests, their minimum effects, and the decision rules were fixed before the analysis (see Appendix A).

**Choices between packages.** To test whether the difficulty lies in the combination itself, we ask the model to choose between two policy packages, each pairing an economic with a social position. A party-line item offers the two usual combinations (EL-SP against ER-ST), and a crossed item offers the two reversed combinations (EL-ST against ER-SP), built from the same {{np_n_pairs}} pairs of economic and social policy passages. The chooser is either one of 20 anonymous House members, half Democrats and half Republicans, or no persona at all. The outcome is the entropy of the model's choice between the two packages, in bits: 0 when the model puts all probability on one package and 1 when it splits evenly. The main contrast is crossed minus party-line entropy. For Democratic and Republican personas, its test, minimum effect, and decision rule were fixed before the analysis; for the no-persona arm, the same contrast is estimated without a registered test. For decided crossed choices, we also describe which position, economic or social, the chosen package satisfies (see Appendix A for the items, the estimation, and the intervals).
