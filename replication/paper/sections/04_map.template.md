# 4. The Map Recovers Aligned Members but Not Cross-Pressured Ones

Each model's map reproduces the main structure of congressional voting. Within each Congress, the economic coordinate correlates with the first DW-NOMINATE dimension at {{r_dim1}} on average. Within each Congress and chamber, the economic coordinate correlates with the economic roll-call scale at {{r_econ}}, and the social coordinate correlates with the abortion scale at {{r_abort}} (Appendix B, Table B1, gives every value by model). In all three models, the parties are farther apart on the economic coordinate than on the social coordinate. The difference between the Republican and Democratic means, in standard deviations of the coordinate, is {{gap_x}} on the economic coordinate and {{gap_y}} on the social coordinate. This ordering matches the history described in Section 1, in which economic issues divided the parties earlier and more consistently than abortion.

The two coordinates, however, carry less separate information than the two directions suggest. Although the directions are perpendicular, each coordinate also follows the other dimension's roll-call scale. The economic coordinate correlates with the abortion scale, and the social coordinate with the economic scale, at {{r_cross_track}}. In every model, the social coordinate follows the economic scale slightly more closely than the abortion scale (Table B1). The models therefore order most members similarly on the two coordinates.

The test is how the map treats members whose two records diverge. Figure 1d compares each row's map quadrant with its voting quadrant. The map places {{recall_aligned}} of aligned rows in their own voting quadrant, but only {{recall_cross}} of cross-pressured rows. Overall, map and votes agree for {{agree}} of rows. Most cross-pressured rows are placed in one of the two aligned quadrants ({{cross_to_aligned}}). This share should not be read as a special pull toward aligned positions, because the map places {{mapped_aligned}} of all rows in aligned quadrants. The map does not separate cross-pressured members from the aligned majority. Because it puts few rows in the cross-pressured quadrants, the low recall there partly reflects how the map's rows are distributed. Two descriptive checks, computed after the main analyses were sealed (Appendix B.5, Table B7), bear on this pattern. A map built from DW-NOMINATE's first and second dimensions, split at the same medians, places {{n1_cross}} of cross-pressured rows in their voting quadrant and {{n1_aligned}} of aligned rows. Its two dimensions are nearly unrelated within a Congress (r = {{n1_r}}), so it puts about half of all rows in the cross-pressured quadrants, whereas the models' two coordinates move together (r = {{xy_r}}). This comparison mainly reflects how differently the two kinds of map distribute members. Restricted to rows whose party the model names correctly, the models place {{n2_aligned}} of aligned rows and {{n2_cross}} of cross-pressured rows in their quadrant, so recognition does not account for the gap. Rows that are economically left and socially traditional are recovered slightly more often than rows that are economically right and socially progressive in every model, by {{elst_minus_ersp}}, but both stay far below the aligned quadrants.

The pattern does not depend on counting members once per Congress or on members close to the medians. Among the {{a12_n}} legislators per voting quadrant selected by their votes, the map places {{a12_aligned}} of the aligned members in their own quadrant and {{a12_cross}} of the cross-pressured members (Figure 1e). Figures 1a to 1c show where each model puts these legislators. In all three maps, most aligned members sit in their expected quadrants, and most cross-pressured members sit near the center or in an adjacent aligned quadrant. Which cross-pressured members are missed differs across models. Qwen places the Republicans Barber Conable and William Frenzel, whose abortion votes were among the most progressive in their chamber, among socially traditional Republicans; Llama places both in their own quadrant, and OLMo places one of them there. Qwen places the Democrat Thomas Eagleton in his own quadrant, while Llama and OLMo do not.
