The third analysis asks whether part of the difficulty lies in the combination of positions itself. The comparison requires personas that keep to their party's line when the line is available. On party-line items, Qwen and OLMo personas choose their own party's package with probability {{own_qo}}. Llama personas do so less reliably, with probabilities of {{own_llama_d}} for Democrats and {{own_llama_r}} for Republicans.

In Qwen and OLMo, crossed choices are clearly more uncertain than party-line choices (Figure 3a). Across the two models and parties, the entropy of party-line choices is close to zero, from {{H_party_qo}} bits, and the entropy of crossed choices ranges from {{H_cross_qo}} bits. The preregistered contrast is {{b_qwen}} bits for Democratic and Republican personas in Qwen and {{b_olmo}} bits in OLMo, and all four tests pass the preregistered rule. Llama goes in the opposite direction ({{b_llama}} bits). Its party-line choices are already uncertain ({{H_party_llama}} bits), and its crossed choices are less so ({{H_cross_llama}} bits). The two models whose personas hold the party line are also the two in which crossed packages are harder.

The same contrast appears without any persona. With no legislator in the prompt, the difference between crossed and party-line entropy is {{np_qwen}} bits in Qwen and {{np_olmo}} bits in OLMo, with 95 percent intervals that resample the economic and social passages. In Llama, it is {{np_llama}} bits. This contrast has no registered test. Crossed combinations are therefore harder for Qwen and OLMo whether or not a legislator is named. This analysis does not test whether the difference is larger with a persona than without one.

When a persona's crossed choice is decided, it usually goes to the package that keeps the persona's economic position (Figure 3b). The share is {{esh_qwen}} for Democratic and Republican personas in Qwen and {{esh_olmo}} in OLMo. In Llama it is {{esh_llama_r}} for Republican personas ({{dec_llama_r}} decided choices) but {{esh_llama_d}} for Democratic personas ({{dec_llama_d}}), so both shares rest on few decided choices. The economic position wins in {{esh_cells_over_half}} of the six combinations of model and party. We report these shares descriptively; no test was registered for them. Table B5 in Appendix B gives all values by model.
